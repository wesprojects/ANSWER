# ANSWER PANEL PLANNER

Plan, specify, build and install Steelcase Answer panel systems, Thin trim first and Oval trim second, from one job file. Draw panel runs on a plan or drop in typicals, set height, stacking, tiles, windows, power and frameless glass per panel, then right-click to add Universal worksurfaces, corner worksurfaces and fixed or mobile pedestals that snap to the panels. Every shared junction, trim, cap, aligner, cantilever, side support bracket, end panel, reinforcing channel, pedestal filler and package is resolved to the exact style number from the February 2015 Answer Solutions Specification Guide. Four stages carry the job from the office to the shop floor: Plan, Specification (buy new, refurbish or stock per line, with package contents spelled out), Shop (upholstery and paint work orders, junction build sheets, field cuts) and Pick & Install (pick list with bins, staging by workstation, installer sheets with plan, elevations and junction-by-junction parts). The specification exports as a SIF file for CAP Worksheet, ProjectSpec and dealer ordering systems, with every finish written as an option pair.

Every reference to the Answer Specification Guide opens that page of the PDF.

## Drawing dimensions

- Junctions are 3" deep (p20) and 1 1/2" along the run: a 48" panel's frame is 46 1/2" between junctions (p30), and two end-of-run junctions make the 3" in-line junction (p45). Skins extend onto the junction and meet the next panel's skin, with only the slotted channel showing between them (p22), so in-line, end-of-run and wall-start junctions are drawn under the skins: a seam line at an in-line junction, the skin running over the end post to the finished trim at a run end. Only corner junctions (L, T, X, V, Y) show their 3" post, and a change of height shows the part of the junction above the lower panel.
- Corner junctions (L, T, X, V, Y) are block-and-post (p21): each panel's module starts at the face of the 3" junction block, so a panel off a corner runs its width plus a corner allowance from the corner's centre to the next junction: 1 1/2" at L, T and X, 0.65" at V and Y. A 48" panel off an L is 49 1/2" from the corner node to the next junction, and its frame is still the 46 1/2" of p30. The allowance is worked out from the worksurface drawings, since the guide prints no single dimension for it: a 48" corner worksurface's arm ends 49 1/2" from the corner (1 1/2" panel face + 1/2" cord drop + 47 1/2", p224, p563) on the next junction, and straights run junction centre to junction centre (p225). The 120° value comes the same way from the 120° corner worksurface (p568). This is what lets a 30"D return butt a straight worksurface at the first junction past a corner (the return's front edge lands 31 1/2" from the corner line, on that junction). Panel positions are recomputed whenever a junction changes type, so adding or removing a leg moves the panels beyond it by the allowance.
- Top caps are the 2 1/4" left of the 6" trim allowance after the 3 3/4" base trim (p19) and sit directly on the skins. They run with the skins, meeting over in-line junctions and over the end post to the end trim. A stacked panel has one top cap, the base panel's, moved to the top of the stack (p32), and the spec notes it. An in-line change of height shows the change-of-height trim on the lower panel's side, its top level with the top cap (p24); glass beside it is shortened (p65). An omitted top cap is drawn as a dashed outline.
- Run ends show the finished end-of-run trim meeting the skins: a flat 1/2" trim on thin (p20, p355), a rounded 1" trim on oval (p92), on the plan, the elevations and the installer sheets.

- Corner junctions show their cap from above (p388): the square 90° corner cap (TS790JC) on L, T and X, the triangular 120° corner cap (TS7120JC) on V and Y. The triangle has 3" sides, so each leg's end face is one side and neighbouring legs meet at its points with no gap; a V also shows its vertical trim on the open side. Caps and trims are drawn in the job's trim finish. Frameless glass and oval top screens show as a line down the top cap.
- `test/caps.js` checks every junction type for gaps between the panels and the cap.
- Worksurface supports are drawn dashed under the worksurface where the spec places them: cantilevers (15 1/2" deep) and center support panels (11") from the panel face at their junctions, end panels at the front ends, side support brackets at return panels and at the rear corner of corner worksurfaces, post legs at the front corner. Tie plates are drawn at every seam. The drawn cantilevers match the UCANT quantity on the specification.

## Drawing and editing

- The ⊕ at a run end has a 20px hit area. Left-click it to add a panel straight on, the same width and height. Right-click it for the compliant directions (straight on, 90° L either side, 120° V either side) or to mark the run as starting at a wall; a right-click never adds a panel (on Windows the menu event comes after the button is released, so the press itself is ignored).
- Drag a panel to move its whole run. When one of the run's junctions comes near another run's junction, it snaps onto it (a green ring shows the target) if the joined junction is one Answer makes: an end of run onto an in-line junction makes a T, onto an end of run an in-line or L, onto an L a T, onto a T an X, and the 120° families alike, with no new rule errors. Anywhere else the run moves on the 6" grid and does not join. Swinging a panel's free end is on the last quarter of the panel, at the tip.
- Add worksurface (toolbar, or W): pick 24, 30 or 18"D and a width (Auto fits the clicked panel; a set width such as 60" spans panels along the run, junction to junction), then click beside a panel for a straight worksurface on that side, or on an L or V junction for a corner worksurface. The panel inspector also has Worksurfaces buttons for side A and side B. On the right-click menu, Add worksurface on this side › 24"D (click the row for a width that fits the panel, or hover it for every width). Placement is fit-checked; a refused size says what it overlaps and which widths fit there.
- Clicking a part in an elevation selects it, centres the plan on it with a pulsing highlight and keeps the page where it was.
- `test/handle.js`, `test/join.js`, `test/corner.js` and `test/elevpick.js` cover these.
- `test/capdxf.js` / `test/capbtn.js` — the CAP DXF export: AutoCAD 2000 audit, every style number counted against the specification, the download buttons.

## DXF export for CAP

Export DXF for CAP writes an AutoCAD 2000 (AC1015) drawing in the form CAP Worksheet reads, decoded from a real CAP drawing: every part is an INSERT of a block named `P_<style number>` carrying CAP's attributes (`CAPPN` style number, `CAPPD` description, `CAPMG` STC, `CAPMC` TSA, `CAPPL` list price, `CAPTG` the visible tag, `CAPQT` 1, `CAPDH`), and every panel is a config block (`P_A36.000000`, labelled "A 36") with its frame or package as the outline and its skins, glass, stacking parts and trims nested as point parts. The junction symbols are CAP's (the 3" corner block with the legs' inner lines, the in-line post, the 1" finished end; the 120° cap for V and Y); frames are drawn 3" deep on the panel's height layer; worksurfaces, pedestals (labelled BBF/FF/BF), cantilevers, center support panels, end panels, post legs, side support brackets and tie plates are drawn where the plan puts them, on CAP's layer names. Every INSERT comes from a Specification line, so `CAPPN` is always the style number on the Specification tab and the number of inserts of a style equals its specified quantity; parts that belong to the job rather than a place (package quantities, aligners, light seals) sit in a "JOB PARTS" row below the plan, and a legend lists each panel config's contents. Nothing is renamed or added for CAP. R12 DXF is refused by CAP ("invalid or incomplete"); R2000 is confirmed accepted. DXF (lines only) keeps the plain R12 drawing. `test/capdxf.js` audits the file with ezdxf and checks every style number's count against the specification; `test/capbtn.js` checks the buttons. The findings and the CAP drawing decode are in `cap/NOTES.md`.

Style numbers the customer's CAP catalog uses that differ from the guide are not substituted: `TS7UCANT` (guide `UCANT`), `USSBR-L`/`USSBR-R` (guide `USSBR`, a pair), `5TS76BPX` (guide `TS76BPX`), and Conklin Avenir worksurfaces and pedestals (the planner specifies Steelcase Universal). To have CAP match those, decide per part and the export can map them.

## 3D blocks in the CAP export (September 25, 2026)

- Beside every `P_<style>` symbol the export now defines its 3D twin `3_<style>`, the body CAP shows when its 3D view swaps the symbols (CAP keeps block definitions that are in the drawing). The bodies are polyface meshes on CAP's 3D layers, built from the guide: base trim 3 3/4" (p58) and the top cap at the actual panel height (p16: 29 1/2", 41 7/8", 48 1/16", 54 1/4", 66 19/32", 78 31/32"), the skins filling from the base trim to the cap's 5/8" lip as the elevation draws them (nominal skins are height less 6", p19), a window tile's frame and pane at its tile height (p59), a stacked panel's tiers at the stacking junction heights with the one top cap moved to the top of the stack (12 3/8", 18 1/2", 24 3/4", p32). Junctions are block-and-post (p21): the 3" block to the post height (p20: 28 7/16" to 77 3/8"), a 3/4" post on each leg out to the skins, the cap from the post height to the top of the top cap, the change-of-height trim on the taller junction's face toward the lower leg (p24), the L and T vertical trim on the block's exposed faces; in-line junctions 1 1/2" along the run (p30); end-of-run posts 3/4" inside the module with the 1/2" finished end beyond (p20); stacking junctions repeat the footprint from the post height. Recessed frameless glass is the p64 glass (9 5/16", 15 1/2", 21 5/8" for the 6", 12" and 18" kits of p396) standing its kit height above the top cap, 3/8" thick on the centreline, 1/16" short of each module line. Worksurfaces are slabs 1 3/16" thick at 28 1/2" (p222). Parts the guide gives no body for (aligners, seals, top cap connectors, stacking bars, power parts, pedestals and supports for now) are points, as in CAP's own library.
- Skins and window panes are drawn as faces, since the guide does not print their thickness; frame members of window tiles are not dimensioned either, so a tile is its outline and pane.
- CAP rebuilds a panel's 3D graphics from the 2D configuration block itself (on opening it reports that the panel builder graphics need updating): each nested symbol stays where the 2D block puts it, is swapped for its `3_` twin and lifted by its CAPDH attribute. The export therefore nests tiles exactly as CAP's own configurations do: the frame at the origin, the side B skin at (0, 3) and the side A skin at (width, 0) turned 180 degrees with CAPDH = 3 3/4" (the base trim, p58), window tiles, stacked tiers and the recessed glass at the origin with CAPDH = their bottom, and every tile body drawn from its own origin. Parts with no body stay point symbols along the panel centreline.
- The owner's two CAP samples (`cap/claude-request-v1.dxf`, a 42" L with a 12" stacker, and `-v2.dxf`, a 42" panel meeting a 54" panel with a glass tile) were decoded for CAP's conventions (`cap/NOTES.md`). CAP's own library drew the 42" end-of-run junction as two points, the same-height L junction as a 0.4" cap at floor level, stacking junctions as a label, the 12" glass screen with the 30"-panel variant so its glass ended flush with the 42" cap, and left the 42" top cap under a stacked window. `test/cap3d.js` audits the export with ezdxf and checks the bodies against those guide numbers; `cap/iso3d.py` renders any CAP DXF's 3D blocks to an isometric SVG for eyeballing.
